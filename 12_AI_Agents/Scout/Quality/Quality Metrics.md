# Quality Metrics

> Quality Metrics of the Scout Strategic Intelligence System

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Authority: Scout Quality

---

# Overview

Quality Metrics defines how organisational quality is measured within the Business Operating System.

Its purpose is to establish a consistent measurement framework that evaluates the effectiveness, reliability and continuous improvement of organisational capability.

Quality should not rely on perception.

It should be supported by meaningful and evidence-based measurement.

---

# Purpose

Quality Metrics exists to provide objective measures of organisational quality.

Its objectives are to:

- Measure organisational performance.
- Monitor quality trends.
- Support continuous improvement.
- Identify quality risks.
- Evaluate organisational capability.
- Strengthen evidence-based decision-making.

Measurement transforms quality from assumption into organisational insight.

---

# Measurement Principles

Every quality metric should follow these principles.

## Measure What Matters

Metrics should support meaningful organisational decisions.

Measurements without business value should be avoided.

---

## Evidence Before Interpretation

Metrics should be based on objective evidence rather than subjective judgement.

---

## Trends Before Snapshots

Long-term trends provide greater organisational insight than isolated measurements.

---

## Improvement Before Comparison

Metrics exist to improve organisational capability rather than compare individuals or departments.

---

## Simplicity Before Complexity

Quality metrics should remain understandable and actionable.

Overly complex measurement reduces organisational usefulness.

---

## Continuous Learning

Every measurement should contribute to organisational learning and Business Operating System evolution.

# Quality Dimensions

Quality should be measured across multiple organisational dimensions.

## Knowledge Quality

Measures the reliability and effectiveness of organisational knowledge.

Example indicators:

- Knowledge accuracy.
- Knowledge consistency.
- Knowledge currency.
- Knowledge completeness.

---

## Intelligence Quality

Measures the effectiveness of research and analysis.

Example indicators:

- Evidence quality.
- Source reliability.
- Analytical consistency.
- Recommendation relevance.

---

## Operational Quality

Measures the consistency and effectiveness of organisational execution.

Example indicators:

- Standards compliance.
- Workflow consistency.
- Process reliability.
- Operational efficiency.

---

## Output Quality

Measures the quality of organisational deliverables.

Example indicators:

- Accuracy.
- Completeness.
- Clarity.
- Actionability.

---

## Decision Quality

Measures how effectively organisational decisions are supported.

Example indicators:

- Evidence utilisation.
- Knowledge alignment.
- Recommendation quality.
- Decision confidence.

---

## System Quality

Measures the overall effectiveness of the Business Operating System.

Example indicators:

- Knowledge growth.
- Standards maturity.
- Governance effectiveness.
- Continuous improvement outcomes.

---

# Metric Characteristics

Effective quality metrics should be:

- Relevant.
- Reliable.
- Repeatable.
- Objective.
- Traceable.
- Actionable.

Metrics should enable organisational improvement rather than merely describe performance.

---

# Measurement Responsibilities

Quality Metrics is responsible for:

- Defining organisational quality indicators.
- Supporting evidence-based measurement.
- Monitoring quality trends.
- Identifying improvement opportunities.
- Supporting strategic decision-making.
- Strengthening the Business Operating System.

---

# Success Criteria

Quality Metrics succeeds when organisational measurement:

- Supports better decisions.
- Improves organisational capability.
- Identifies meaningful trends.
- Encourages continuous improvement.
- Strengthens organisational trust.
- Enables long-term organisational excellence.

Success is measured by the value metrics provide to organisational improvement rather than the number of metrics collected.

---

# Relationship with Other Quality Domains

Quality Framework defines the organisational quality model.

Quality Standards define quality expectations.

Quality Assurance builds quality into organisational processes.

Quality Review evaluates organisational quality.

Continuous Verification maintains organisational quality.

Quality Metrics measures organisational performance and improvement.

Together these domains establish a complete organisational quality management system.

---

# Philosophy

Quality should be observable.

What can be observed can be understood.

What can be understood can be improved.

Quality Metrics exists to transform organisational quality into measurable organisational capability.

Every meaningful metric supports better decisions.

Every better decision strengthens organisational capability.

Every improvement strengthens the Business Operating System.

Quality is not measured for reporting.

It is measured for learning, improvement and long-term organisational excellence.
