Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

---

# Overview

Repository Quality defines the principles, standards and practices that ensure organisational assets remain accurate, reliable and reusable throughout their lifecycle.

Its purpose is to maintain the long-term integrity, consistency and value of the Repository as the trusted source of organisational assets within the Business Operating System.

Repository quality is achieved through continuous governance rather than one-time validation.

---

# Purpose

Repository Quality exists to maintain the quality of organisational assets.

Its objectives are to:

- Maintain repository integrity.
- Protect asset quality.
- Support long-term reuse.
- Reduce repository degradation.
- Strengthen organisational trust.
- Preserve organisational capability.

High-quality repositories enable sustainable organisational growth.

---

# Repository Quality Principles

Repository Quality follows these principles.

## Quality Before Quantity

The value of a repository is determined by the quality of its assets rather than the number of assets it contains.

---

## Integrity Before Convenience

Repository assets should remain accurate, complete and trustworthy.

Convenience should never compromise organisational integrity.

---

## Currency Before History

Assets should remain current and actively maintained.

Historical assets should be clearly identified through lifecycle governance.

---

## Consistency Before Variation

Repository assets should follow approved organisational standards, terminology and documentation practices.

Consistency improves discoverability and reuse.

---

## Reuse Before Recreation

Repository quality increases when approved assets are consistently reused across the organisation.

Unnecessary duplication reduces repository quality.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Repository quality should improve through governance, review and organisational learning.

Repository quality is never considered permanently complete.

# Repository Quality Dimensions

Repository assets should be evaluated across multiple quality dimensions.

## Accuracy

The asset reflects approved organisational knowledge and verified information.

---

## Completeness

The asset contains sufficient information for its intended organisational purpose.

---

## Consistency

The asset follows approved standards, terminology and documentation conventions.

---

## Currency

The asset remains up to date and reflects current organisational practices.

---

## Traceability

Ownership, approval status, version history and supporting evidence are clearly recorded.

---

## Reusability

The asset can be applied across multiple organisational situations without unnecessary modification.

---

## Discoverability

The asset can be efficiently located through repository structure, classification and metadata.

---

## Maintainability

The asset can be updated without reducing repository integrity or organisational consistency.

- Archive management.
- Improvement recommendations.

These activities support the long-term health of the Repository.

# Repository Responsibilities

Repository Quality is responsible for:

- Maintaining repository integrity.
- Supporting asset quality.
- Improving repository consistency.
- Protecting organisational assets.
- Enabling sustainable asset reuse.
- Supporting Business Operating System governance.

Repository Quality protects the long-term value of organisational assets.

# Success Criteria

Repository Quality succeeds when repository assets are:

- Accurate.
- Complete.
- Consistent.
- Current.
- Traceable.
- Reusable.
- Discoverable.
- Maintainable.

Success is measured by the sustained quality and usefulness of organisational assets rather than repository growth.
