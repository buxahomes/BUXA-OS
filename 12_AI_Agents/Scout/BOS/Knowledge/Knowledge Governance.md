# Knowledge Governance

> Knowledge Governance of the Scout Strategic Intelligence System

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Authority: Scout Knowledge

---

# Overview

Knowledge Governance defines the ownership, authority and decision-making responsibilities for organisational knowledge within the Business Operating System.

Its purpose is to ensure that organisational knowledge remains trustworthy, well managed and aligned with business objectives through clear governance and accountable stewardship.

Knowledge should be governed as a strategic organisational asset rather than maintained through informal individual practices.

---

# Purpose

Knowledge Governance exists to establish clear accountability for organisational knowledge.

Its objectives are to:

- Define knowledge ownership.
- Establish approval authority.
- Protect organisational consistency.
- Ensure responsible knowledge maintenance.
- Support transparent decision-making.
- Strengthen long-term organisational capability.

Every approved knowledge asset should have clearly defined ownership and governance responsibilities.

---

# Governance Principles

Every governance activity should follow these principles.

## Accountability Before Authority

Every knowledge asset should have an accountable owner.

Authority without accountability weakens organisational governance.

---

## Human Responsibility Before Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence may assist with creating, analysing and maintaining knowledge.

Only authorised human decision-makers may approve, revise or retire permanent organisational knowledge.

---

## Ownership Before Modification

Knowledge should only be modified through authorised governance processes.

Unauthorised changes reduce organisational trust.

---

## Transparency Before Complexity

Governance responsibilities should be clearly defined and easily understood.

Transparent governance improves organisational confidence.

---

## Consistency Before Individual Preference

Knowledge decisions should support organisational standards rather than personal working styles.

---

## Governance Supports Improvement

Governance should enable continuous improvement while preserving organisational stability.

Well-governed knowledge evolves with purpose rather than through uncontrolled change.

---

# Governance Roles

Knowledge governance includes the following responsibilities.

## Knowledge Owner

Responsible for the long-term integrity and business relevance of a knowledge asset.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Defining scope.
- Maintaining accuracy.
- Initiating updates.
- Supporting organisational use.

---

## Knowledge Contributor

Creates or improves knowledge in accordance with approved standards.

Contributors support organisational capability but do not approve permanent knowledge independently.

---

## Knowledge Reviewer

Evaluates knowledge for technical accuracy, completeness and alignment with organisational standards.

Review ensures quality before approval.

---

## Knowledge Approver

Authorises knowledge for organisational use.

Approval confirms that knowledge meets organisational standards and becomes an approved reference within the Business Operating System.

---

## Knowledge User

Applies approved knowledge in business operations, decision-making and AI-supported activities.

Users should rely on approved knowledge and report opportunities for improvement when appropriate.

# Governance Responsibilities

Knowledge Governance is responsible for:

- Assigning ownership.
- Managing approvals.
- Supporting structured review.
- Protecting organisational consistency.
- Coordinating knowledge changes.
- Preserving governance records.
- Supporting continuous improvement.

---

# Governance Decisions

Governance applies to decisions such as:

- Creating new knowledge assets.
- Revising existing standards.
- Approving organisational knowledge.
- Retiring obsolete knowledge.
- Resolving conflicting knowledge.
- Assigning ownership.
- Defining review responsibilities.

Every significant knowledge decision should be traceable.

---

# Decision Authority

Knowledge decisions should follow an appropriate governance hierarchy.

Knowledge Contributor

↓

Knowledge Reviewer

↓

Knowledge Approver

↓

Approved Organisational Knowledge

Strategic organisational knowledge should require appropriate business approval before becoming permanent.

---

# Governance Records

Governance activities should maintain records of:

- Ownership.
- Approval status.
- Review history.
- Version history.
- Significant decisions.
- Retirement decisions.

Governance records improve organisational transparency and accountability.

---

# Key Responsibilities

Knowledge Governance is responsible for:

- Defining governance responsibilities.
- Maintaining ownership.
- Coordinating approvals.
- Supporting organisational accountability.
- Protecting knowledge integrity.
- Strengthening Business Operating System governance.

---

# Success Criteria

Knowledge Governance succeeds when organisational knowledge is:

- Clearly owned.
- Properly approved.
- Responsibly maintained.
- Transparently governed.
- Consistently applied.
- Continuously improved.

Success is measured by organisational trust, accountability and governance effectiveness rather than the number of governance activities performed.

# Philosophy

Knowledge creates value only when it can be trusted.

Trust requires accountability.

Accountability requires governance.

Governance ensures that organisational knowledge remains accurate, consistent and aligned with business objectives.

The Business Operating System does not depend on individual memory.

It depends on knowledge that is clearly owned, responsibly governed and continuously strengthened.

Good governance protects today's knowledge.

Excellent governance enables tomorrow's capability.
