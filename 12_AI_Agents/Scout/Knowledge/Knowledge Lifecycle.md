# Knowledge Lifecycle

> Knowledge Lifecycle of the Scout Strategic Intelligence System

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Authority: Scout Knowledge

---

# Overview

Knowledge Lifecycle defines how organisational knowledge is created, reviewed, approved, maintained and retired within the Business Operating System.

Its purpose is to ensure that organisational knowledge remains accurate, relevant and continuously valuable throughout its entire lifecycle.

Knowledge should evolve through structured governance rather than uncontrolled accumulation.

---

# Purpose

Knowledge Lifecycle exists to establish a consistent process for managing organisational knowledge from its creation to its retirement.

Its objectives are to:

- Support controlled knowledge creation.
- Ensure appropriate review and approval.
- Maintain knowledge accuracy.
- Encourage continuous improvement.
- Preserve organisational memory.
- Prevent outdated or conflicting knowledge.

Every knowledge asset should follow the same lifecycle regardless of its domain.

---

# Lifecycle Principles

Every knowledge asset should follow these principles.

## Purpose Before Creation

Knowledge should be created only when it provides clear organisational value.

Unnecessary documentation increases organisational complexity.

---

## Review Before Approval

Knowledge should be reviewed before becoming an approved organisational standard.

Review improves reliability.

---

## Approval Before Adoption

Only approved knowledge should be applied across the organisation.

Draft knowledge should not replace established standards.

---

## Maintenance Before Obsolescence

Knowledge should be reviewed and updated before it becomes outdated.

Continuous maintenance preserves organisational trust.

---

## Evolution Before Replacement

Approved knowledge should evolve through controlled improvement whenever practical.

Complete replacement should occur only when necessary.

---

## Preservation Before Deletion

Historical knowledge should be archived where it provides future organisational value.

Organisational learning should not be lost.

# Lifecycle Stages

## Identify Need

A business requirement or knowledge gap is identified.

Knowledge creation should always be purpose-driven.

---

## Create Draft

Initial knowledge is documented according to approved organisational standards.

---

## Technical Review

Subject matter experts verify technical accuracy and completeness.

---

## Business Review

Knowledge is evaluated for alignment with organisational strategy, standards and operational needs.

---

## Approval

Authorised reviewers approve the knowledge for organisational use.

Only approved knowledge becomes part of the Business Operating System.

---

## Published Knowledge

Approved knowledge becomes available for organisational use.

It now serves as the authoritative reference for its defined scope.

---

## Operational Use

Knowledge is applied across business operations, products and AI capabilities.

Operational use provides practical feedback for future improvement.

---

## Periodic Review

Knowledge should be reviewed at appropriate intervals or when significant business changes occur.

Review ensures continued relevance.

---

## Update or Revise

Knowledge is improved when new evidence, organisational decisions or changing requirements justify revision.

All revisions should preserve version history.

---

## Archive or Retire

Knowledge that is no longer applicable should be archived or formally retired.

Archived knowledge remains available for historical reference where appropriate.

---

# Lifecycle Triggers

Knowledge review may be triggered by:

- New business strategy.
- Product changes.
- PDMS updates.
- Brand evolution.
- Regulatory changes.
- Customer feedback.
- Lessons learned.
- Quality findings.
- Executive decisions.

Knowledge should evolve in response to meaningful organisational change.

---

# Version Management

Every approved knowledge asset should include:

- Version number.
- Approval status.
- Effective date.
- Revision history.
- Responsible owner.

Version management improves traceability and organisational confidence.

---

# Key Responsibilities

Knowledge Lifecycle is responsible for:

- Managing knowledge creation.
- Supporting structured review.
- Coordinating approval.
- Maintaining organisational knowledge.
- Preserving historical knowledge.
- Supporting continuous improvement.
- Strengthening the Business Operating System.

---

# Success Criteria

Knowledge Lifecycle succeeds when organisational knowledge is:

- Purposeful.
- Current.
- Well maintained.
- Properly governed.
- Continuously improved.
- Reliably accessible.

Success is measured by the long-term effectiveness of organisational knowledge rather than the speed of document creation.

# Philosophy

Knowledge should never remain static.

Every standard begins as an idea.

Every idea becomes knowledge through review and approval.

Every approved knowledge asset should continue to improve through experience and organisational learning.

The Business Operating System grows through disciplined evolution rather than uncontrolled expansion.

Knowledge is not created once.

It is continuously refined, maintained and strengthened throughout its lifecycle.

Every improvement increases organisational capability.

Every lifecycle strengthens the future of BUXA.
