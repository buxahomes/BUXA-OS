# Knowledge Classification

> Knowledge Classification of the Scout Strategic Intelligence System

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Authority: Scout Knowledge

---

# Overview

Knowledge Classification defines how organisational knowledge is categorised within the Business Operating System.

Its purpose is to establish a consistent classification framework that improves knowledge organisation, governance, retrieval and application.

Classification describes the purpose and nature of knowledge rather than its storage location.

---

# Purpose

Knowledge Classification exists to organise organisational knowledge into meaningful categories.

Its objectives are to:

- Improve knowledge discoverability.
- Support consistent governance.
- Enable appropriate knowledge usage.
- Simplify organisational navigation.
- Strengthen AI-assisted retrieval.
- Improve long-term organisational scalability.

Every approved knowledge asset should belong to at least one defined classification.

---

# Classification Principles

Every knowledge asset should follow these principles.

## Purpose Before Category

Knowledge should be classified according to its organisational purpose.

Classification should reflect how knowledge is intended to be used.

---

## Meaning Before Structure

Classification should describe the meaning of knowledge rather than the folder in which it is stored.

---

## Consistency Before Flexibility

The same type of knowledge should always use the same classification across the Business Operating System.

---

## Simplicity Before Complexity

Classification should remain easy to understand and consistently applied.

An effective system uses only the categories required to support organisational capability.

---

## Scalability Before Completeness

The classification framework should support future organisational growth without requiring major redesign.

---

## Reusability Before Duplication

Knowledge should be reusable across multiple business capabilities regardless of its classification.

Classification supports discovery rather than restricting usage.

---

# Knowledge Classifications

Organisational knowledge may be classified into the following categories.

## Strategic Knowledge

Knowledge that defines long-term organisational direction.

Examples include:

- Business Strategy
- Brand Strategy
- Product Strategy
- AI Strategy

---

## Governance Knowledge

Knowledge that defines authority, responsibilities and organisational rules.

Examples include:

- Constitutions
- Governance Standards
- Policies
- Responsibilities

---

## Standard Knowledge

Knowledge that defines approved organisational standards.

Examples include:

- Product Standards
- Brand Standards
- PDMS Standards
- Quality Standards

---

## Operational Knowledge

Knowledge supporting day-to-day business execution.

Examples include:

- Procedures
- Workflows
- Operating Guides
- Checklists

---

## Technical Knowledge

Knowledge describing technical specifications and implementation details.

Examples include:

- Product Passports
- Specifications
- Installation Guides
- Technical References

---

## Reference Knowledge

Knowledge used for consultation and ongoing organisational learning.

Examples include:

- Glossaries
- Templates
- FAQs
- Design Guides
- Reference Manuals

---

## Historical Knowledge

Knowledge preserved for organisational memory and traceability.

Examples include:

- Archived Standards
- Previous Versions
- Historical Reports
- Lessons Learned

---

# Applying Classification

Every approved knowledge asset should have an assigned classification.

Where appropriate, a knowledge asset may include one primary classification and one or more secondary classifications.

Primary classification defines the asset's principal organisational purpose.

Secondary classifications support retrieval and cross-functional use.

---

# Benefits of Classification

Effective classification improves:

- Knowledge retrieval.
- Organisational consistency.
- Governance.
- AI-assisted search.
- Knowledge maintenance.
- Cross-functional collaboration.

Classification enables organisational understanding at scale.

---

# Key Responsibilities

Knowledge Classification is responsible for:

- Defining classification categories.
- Maintaining classification consistency.
- Supporting knowledge organisation.
- Improving discoverability.
- Strengthening governance.
- Supporting Business Operating System scalability.

---

# Success Criteria

Knowledge Classification succeeds when organisational knowledge is:

- Clearly categorised.
- Easily discoverable.
- Consistently classified.
- Simple to navigate.
- Effectively reused.
- Well governed.

Success is measured by the ease with which knowledge can be understood, located and applied.

# Philosophy

Classification creates organisational clarity.

Clarity improves understanding.

Understanding improves decision-making.

Every knowledge asset should have a clearly defined organisational purpose.

Classification is not about storing documents.

It is about making organisational knowledge understandable, reusable and valuable.

Well-classified knowledge becomes organisational intelligence.

Organisational intelligence strengthens the Business Operating System.
