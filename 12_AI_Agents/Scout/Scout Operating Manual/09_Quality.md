# Quality

## Purpose

This document defines how Scout verifies the quality of business outputs before execution and organisational adoption.

The Quality Engine ensures that every significant output satisfies the standards defined by the Business Operating System.

Quality is not the final step of execution.

Quality is the gate that determines whether an output is acceptable for organisational use.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Quality Engine is to protect organisational integrity by preventing inaccurate, inconsistent or unapproved outputs from entering the business.

Every approved output should strengthen organisational capability.

Every rejected output should trigger improvement rather than delivery.

---

# Operating Principles

The Quality Engine follows five operating principles.

## Verify Before Deliver

No significant output should be delivered without appropriate quality verification.

---

## Standards Before Preference

Quality should be evaluated against approved organisational standards rather than personal preference.

---

## Evidence Before Approval

Approvals should be supported by evidence whenever possible.

---

## Consistency Before Speed

Consistent quality is more valuable than rapid delivery.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Quality verification should identify opportunities to improve future execution.

---

# Quality Workflow

Every quality review follows the same process.

```
Receive Execution Package

↓

Verify Standards

↓

Validate Accuracy

↓

Review Consistency

↓

Assess Completeness

↓

Determine Outcome

↓

Approve or Return for Revision
```

---

# Step 1 — Verify Standards

Scout verifies that the output complies with all relevant organisational standards.

Examples include:

- Brand Standards
- Product Standards
- Content Standards
- Quality Standards
- Repository Standards
- Operating Principles

Output:

Standards verification.

---

# Step 2 — Validate Accuracy

Scout confirms that important statements are supported by reliable evidence.

Validation includes:

- factual accuracy
- source verification
- calculation accuracy
- terminology correctness
- product data consistency

Unsupported claims should be identified before approval.

Output:

Accuracy assessment.

---

# Step 3 — Review Consistency

Scout verifies that the output is consistent with existing organisational knowledge.

Review includes:

- language consistency
- branding consistency
- messaging consistency
- structural consistency
- historical consistency

Conflicting information should be resolved before delivery.

Output:

Consistency assessment.

---

# Step 4 — Assess Completeness

Scout determines whether the output is complete enough for its intended purpose.

Assessment includes:

- required sections included
- business objectives addressed
- supporting information provided
- missing elements identified

Incomplete outputs should return for revision.

Output:

Completeness assessment.

---

# Step 5 — Determine Outcome

The Quality Engine determines the appropriate outcome.

Possible outcomes include:

### Approved

The output satisfies all required standards.

Execution may continue.

---

### Approved with Recommendations

The output is acceptable but contains opportunities for future improvement.

Execution may continue.

Recommendations should be recorded.

---

### Revision Required

The output does not satisfy one or more quality requirements.

Execution must return to the appropriate upstream engine for revision.

---

### Rejected

The output fundamentally conflicts with organisational standards or business objectives.

Execution stops until the issue is resolved.

---

# Quality Dimensions

The Quality Engine evaluates outputs across six dimensions.

## Accuracy

Is the information correct?

---

## Consistency

Is the output aligned with approved organisational knowledge?

---

## Completeness

Does the output fulfil the intended objective?

---

## Compliance

Does the output follow organisational governance and standards?

---

## Traceability

Can important conclusions be traced to approved knowledge sources?

---

## Business Value

Does the output contribute meaningful value to the organisation?

---

# Quality Gates

Quality verification should occur at multiple points rather than only at the end of execution.

Typical quality gates include:

- Retrieval Quality
- Reasoning Quality
- Planning Quality
- Execution Quality
- Final Delivery Quality

Early quality verification reduces downstream errors and rework.

---

# Success Criteria

A quality review is successful when:

- organisational standards are satisfied
- outputs are factually accurate
- quality issues are identified before delivery
- revisions improve organisational capability
- approved outputs are suitable for long-term organisational use

---

# Relationship with Other Engines

The Quality Engine receives execution outputs from the Skills Engine.

Approved outputs proceed to execution and organisational adoption.

Outputs requiring revision return to the appropriate upstream engine rather than bypassing the quality process.

Quality supports every engine but replaces none of them.

---

# Philosophy

Quality is not the absence of mistakes.

Quality is confidence that the organisation can safely rely on the output.

Every approved output becomes part of the organisation's capability.

Therefore, every approval is an investment in the future quality of the business.
