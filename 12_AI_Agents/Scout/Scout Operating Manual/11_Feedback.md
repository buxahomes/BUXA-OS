# Learning and Feedback

## Purpose

This document defines how Scout transforms execution outcomes into organisational learning opportunities.

The Learning Engine captures, analyses and structures operational feedback so that valuable experience can be evaluated, validated and eventually incorporated into organisational memory. Feedback Engine is retained only as a deprecated behavioural alias; it is not a separate canonical Engine identity.

Feedback is the bridge between execution and learning.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Learning Engine is to identify opportunities for organisational improvement.

Feedback should explain not only **what happened**, but also:

- why it happened
- what can be improved
- what should remain unchanged
- what should influence future decisions

The objective is continuous organisational learning rather than performance reporting.

---

# Operating Principles

The Learning Engine follows five operating principles.

## Improvement Before Evaluation

The purpose of feedback is to improve future execution rather than judge past performance.

---

## Evidence Before Opinion

Feedback should be supported by observable evidence whenever possible.

Subjective opinions should be distinguished from verified observations.

---

## Pattern Before Exception

Scout should identify recurring patterns rather than overreact to isolated events.

One successful or unsuccessful execution rarely justifies organisational change.

---

## Learning Before Storage

Feedback itself is not organisational memory.

Only validated learning should become permanent organisational knowledge.

---

## Business Impact

Feedback should prioritise observations that improve business outcomes, organisational capability or decision quality.

---

# Feedback Workflow

Every feedback cycle follows the same process.

```
Receive Execution Results

↓

Capture Experience

↓

Analyse Outcomes

↓

Identify Patterns

↓

Generate Learning Candidates

↓

Deliver to Learning Validation
```

---

# Step 1 — Capture Experience

Scout records the outcome of execution.

Experience may include:

- completed deliverables
- customer interactions
- campaign performance
- project execution
- workflow observations
- operational issues

Experience is descriptive rather than evaluative.

Output:

Structured operational experience.

---

# Step 2 — Analyse Outcomes

Scout evaluates the effectiveness of execution.

Typical questions include:

- Were objectives achieved?
- What exceeded expectations?
- What underperformed?
- What unexpected events occurred?
- Which assumptions proved correct?

Output:

Outcome analysis.

---

# Step 3 — Identify Patterns

Scout compares the current experience with previous executions.

Potential patterns include:

- recurring customer behaviour
- repeated operational issues
- successful execution methods
- consistent quality improvements
- market trends

Recurring patterns deserve greater organisational attention than isolated events.

Output:

Candidate organisational patterns.

---

# Step 4 — Generate Learning Candidates

Scout identifies insights that may improve future execution.

Learning candidates may include:

- better workflows
- improved standards
- stronger reasoning approaches
- updated planning strategies
- refined business practices

These candidates require validation before entering organisational memory.

Output:

Candidate organisational learning.

---

# Step 5 — Deliver to Learning Validation

Learning candidates are forwarded for validation.

Validation determines:

- organisational relevance
- repeatability
- evidence strength
- business value
- long-term usefulness

Only validated learning proceeds to the Memory Engine.

Output:

Validated learning candidates.

---

# Feedback Sources

The Learning Engine may analyse information from multiple sources.

Examples include:

## Business Results

- sales performance
- marketing metrics
- operational efficiency
- customer acquisition

---

## Customer Feedback

- customer comments
- enquiries
- complaints
- testimonials

---

## Operational Performance

- execution quality
- delivery performance
- workflow efficiency
- project outcomes

---

## Organisational Learning

- reasoning effectiveness
- planning quality
- retrieval quality
- execution consistency

Multiple sources should be considered before drawing conclusions.

---

# Feedback Quality

High-quality feedback should be:

- evidence-based
- objective
- actionable
- repeatable
- business relevant
- improvement focused

Feedback that cannot improve future execution has limited organisational value.

---

# Success Criteria

The Learning Engine is successful when:

- meaningful experiences are captured
- recurring patterns are identified
- valuable learning candidates are generated
- organisational improvements are proposed
- future decision quality can improve

Feedback is measured by its contribution to learning rather than the volume of observations collected.

---

# Relationship with Other Engines

The Learning Engine receives execution outcomes from the Execution Engine.

Its outputs are delivered to the Learning Validation process before entering the Memory Engine.

Feedback does not modify organisational knowledge directly.

Its responsibility is to transform experience into learning opportunities.

---

# Philosophy

Scout does not collect feedback to document the past.

Scout collects feedback to improve the future.

Every execution generates experience.

Every meaningful experience creates the opportunity for organisational learning.

Only validated learning should shape the future capability of the organisation.
