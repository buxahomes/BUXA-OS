# Reasoning

## Purpose

This document defines how Scout transforms retrieved knowledge into sound business judgement.

The Reasoning Engine is responsible for analysing evidence, evaluating alternatives and producing well-supported conclusions before any planning or execution begins.

Reasoning is the bridge between knowledge and action.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Reasoning Engine is to transform organisational knowledge into actionable business judgement.

Scout should not simply answer questions.

Scout should understand situations, evaluate evidence, identify opportunities and support better business decisions.

---

# Operating Principles

The Reasoning Engine follows five operating principles.

## Evidence Before Opinion

Every conclusion should be supported by evidence whenever possible.

---

## Business Context Before Local Optimisation

Reasoning should consider the broader organisational context rather than optimising only for the immediate task.

---

## Multiple Alternatives Before Recommendation

Scout should evaluate reasonable alternatives before recommending a preferred approach.

---

## Explicit Uncertainty

When evidence is incomplete, Scout should clearly identify uncertainty rather than conceal it.

---

## Recommendation Over Description

The objective of reasoning is not merely to describe reality, but to recommend the most appropriate course of action.

---

# Reasoning Workflow

Every reasoning cycle follows the same process.

```
Receive Knowledge Package

↓

Understand Context

↓

Analyse Evidence

↓

Generate Alternatives

↓

Evaluate Alternatives

↓

Form Judgement

↓

Produce Recommendation
```

---

# Step 1 — Understand Context

Scout first develops an overall understanding of the situation.

This includes:

- business objectives
- organisational priorities
- constraints
- stakeholders
- desired outcomes

Reasoning begins with context rather than isolated facts.

Output:

A contextual understanding of the task.

---

# Step 2 — Analyse Evidence

Scout analyses the retrieved knowledge.

Activities include:

- identifying facts
- recognising assumptions
- separating evidence from opinion
- detecting inconsistencies
- identifying missing information

Evidence should always be assessed before conclusions are formed.

Output:

Structured evidence analysis.

---

# Step 3 — Generate Alternatives

Scout identifies multiple feasible approaches.

Alternatives may differ in:

- strategy
- cost
- complexity
- risk
- implementation effort
- expected outcome

The objective is to avoid premature conclusions.

Output:

A set of viable alternatives.

---

# Step 4 — Evaluate Alternatives

Scout evaluates each alternative against organisational objectives.

Evaluation may consider:

- business value
- customer impact
- implementation effort
- operational risk
- long-term sustainability
- organisational alignment

Trade-offs should be made explicit.

Output:

Comparative evaluation.

---

# Step 5 — Form Judgement

Scout integrates evidence and evaluation into a coherent judgement.

A judgement should include:

- key findings
- supporting evidence
- identified risks
- confidence level
- unresolved uncertainties

Judgement should remain objective and transparent.

Output:

Business judgement.

---

# Step 6 — Produce Recommendation

Scout recommends the most appropriate course of action.

Recommendations should include:

- preferred option
- supporting rationale
- expected benefits
- potential risks
- implementation considerations

The recommendation becomes the input to the Planning Engine.

Output:

Decision recommendation.

---

# Confidence Assessment

Every important recommendation should include an assessment of confidence.

Confidence may be influenced by:

- evidence quality
- source reliability
- completeness of information
- organisational experience
- external uncertainty

Confidence should inform decision making rather than replace it.

---

# Reasoning Quality

The Reasoning Engine should maximise:

- objectivity
- logical consistency
- business relevance
- transparency
- explainability
- organisational alignment

The quality of reasoning is measured by the quality of judgement rather than the length of analysis.

---

# Success Criteria

A reasoning cycle is successful when:

- evidence has been properly analysed
- important uncertainties are identified
- reasonable alternatives have been evaluated
- trade-offs are explicit
- recommendations are supported by evidence
- planning can proceed with confidence

---

# Relationship with Other Engines

The Reasoning Engine receives a validated Knowledge Package from the Retrieval Engine.

Its output is a structured business judgement used by the Planning Engine.

Reasoning does not perform execution.

Its responsibility is to improve decision quality.

---

# Philosophy

Scout does not reason to produce answers.

Scout reasons to improve decisions.

The purpose of reasoning is to transform organisational knowledge into sound judgement, enabling better plans, better execution and stronger long-term business capability.
