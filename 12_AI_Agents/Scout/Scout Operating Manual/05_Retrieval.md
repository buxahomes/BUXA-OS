# Retrieval

## Purpose

This document defines how Scout acquires the knowledge required to perform business tasks.

The Retrieval Engine is responsible for constructing a complete and reliable knowledge context before reasoning begins.

Retrieval is the foundation of every decision.

Without sufficient knowledge, reliable reasoning is impossible.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Retrieval Engine is to transform a task into a verified knowledge package.

It should retrieve the minimum knowledge required to maximise decision quality while avoiding unnecessary information.

Retrieval is not information collection.

Retrieval is contextual understanding.

---

# Operating Principles

The Retrieval Engine follows five operating principles.

## Retrieve Before Generate

Scout must always retrieve approved organisational knowledge before generating new content.

---

## Organisational Knowledge First

Internal organisational knowledge always takes priority over external information whenever applicable.

---

## Evidence Over Volume

More information does not necessarily improve decisions.

Only relevant and reliable knowledge should be retrieved.

---

## Context Before Detail

Scout should first understand the overall business context before retrieving detailed information.

---

## Traceable Sources

Every important conclusion should be traceable to one or more knowledge sources.

---

# Retrieval Workflow

Every retrieval cycle follows the same process.

```

Understand Task

↓

Identify Knowledge Requirements

↓

Locate Sources

↓

Evaluate Relevance

↓

Assemble Knowledge Package

↓

Deliver to Reasoning Engine

```text

---

# Step 1 — Understand the Task

Scout first determines what problem needs to be solved.

The objective is not to retrieve information immediately, but to understand the business intent.

Questions include:

- What is the real objective?
- What business domain is involved?
- What decisions must be made?
- What outputs are expected?

Output:

A structured retrieval objective.

---

# Step 2 — Identify Knowledge Requirements

Scout determines which knowledge is required.

Knowledge may include:

- business knowledge
- product knowledge
- customer knowledge
- competitor knowledge
- project knowledge
- market knowledge
- organisational standards
- historical decisions

The objective is to retrieve only knowledge that contributes to better reasoning.

Output:

Knowledge requirements.

---

# Step 3 — Locate Knowledge Sources

Scout identifies where knowledge should be retrieved.

Typical sources include:

### Internal Sources

- Business Operating System
- Repository
- Organisational Memory
- Product Passports
- Approved Standards
- Previous Projects

### External Sources

- Official websites
- Government publications
- Industry standards
- Market reports
- Academic research
- Authoritative public information

Internal knowledge should always be evaluated before external knowledge.

Output:

Relevant knowledge sources.

---

# Step 4 — Evaluate Relevance

Not every retrieved document should be used.

Scout evaluates retrieved knowledge according to:

- relevance
- authority
- accuracy
- recency
- organisational approval
- completeness

Knowledge failing these criteria should be excluded.

Output:

Validated knowledge.

---

# Step 5 — Assemble Knowledge Package

Scout organises retrieved knowledge into a coherent context.

The package should include:

- business context
- relevant facts
- organisational standards
- assumptions
- uncertainties
- supporting evidence

The objective is to prepare reasoning rather than overwhelm it.

Output:

Knowledge Package.

---

# Knowledge Priority

When multiple sources exist, Scout retrieves knowledge according to the following priority.

1. Business Operating System
2. Approved Repository Assets
3. Organisational Memory
4. Previous Approved Outputs
5. External Authoritative Sources
6. General Public Information

Higher-priority knowledge overrides lower-priority knowledge when conflicts occur.

---

# Retrieval Quality

The Retrieval Engine should maximise:

- relevance
- reliability
- traceability
- completeness
- contextual understanding

The objective is not to retrieve the largest amount of knowledge, but the most useful knowledge.

---

# Success Criteria

A retrieval cycle is successful when:

- all required organisational knowledge has been considered
- knowledge is relevant to the task
- important sources are traceable
- uncertainty is identified
- reasoning can proceed without unnecessary information gaps

---

# Relationship with Other Engines

The Retrieval Engine receives tasks from the Input Engine.

Its output becomes the sole input to the Reasoning Engine.

The quality of reasoning depends directly on the quality of retrieval.

---

# Philosophy

Scout does not retrieve documents.

Scout retrieves organisational understanding.

The purpose of retrieval is not to collect information, but to provide the knowledge context required for intelligent reasoning and high-quality business decisions.
