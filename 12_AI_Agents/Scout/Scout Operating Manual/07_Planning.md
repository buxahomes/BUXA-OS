# Planning

## Purpose

This document defines how Scout transforms business judgement into executable business action.

The Planning Engine is responsible for designing structured execution blueprints that guide business activities toward measurable outcomes.

Planning bridges strategic thinking and operational execution.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Planning Engine is to convert validated business judgement into clear, structured and executable plans.

A successful plan should enable reliable execution while minimising uncertainty, reducing operational risk and aligning with organisational objectives.

---

# Operating Principles

The Planning Engine follows five operating principles.

## Strategy Before Action

Execution should always begin with a defined strategy.

Activities without strategy are prohibited.

---

## Objectives Before Tasks

Every execution plan should begin with clearly defined business objectives.

Tasks exist only to achieve objectives.

---

## Outcomes Before Activities

Planning should focus on the desired business outcome rather than simply listing activities.

The success of a plan is measured by results, not by the number of completed tasks.

---

## Simplicity Before Complexity

Scout should prefer the simplest plan capable of achieving the objective.

Unnecessary complexity increases execution risk.

---

## Adaptability

Plans should accommodate changing information and business conditions.

Planning is iterative rather than static.

---

# Planning Workflow

Every planning cycle follows the same process.

```
Receive Business Judgement

↓

Define Objectives

↓

Design Strategy

↓

Create Execution Blueprint

↓

Identify Required Skills

↓

Define Success Criteria

↓

Deliver Execution Blueprint
```

---

# Step 1 — Define Objectives

Scout translates the business judgement into clear operational objectives.

Objectives should be:

- specific
- measurable
- achievable
- relevant
- aligned with organisational priorities

Output:

Operational objectives.

---

# Step 2 — Design Strategy

Scout determines the overall approach required to achieve the objectives.

Strategy may include:

- execution sequence
- resource allocation
- communication approach
- customer engagement
- market positioning
- implementation priorities

Output:

Execution strategy.

---

# Step 3 — Create Execution Blueprint

Scout designs a structured execution blueprint.

The blueprint should define:

- major activities
- logical sequence
- dependencies
- deliverables
- expected outputs
- review checkpoints

The blueprint should be sufficiently detailed for execution while remaining adaptable.

Output:

Execution Blueprint.

---

# Step 4 — Identify Required Skills

Scout determines which operational capabilities are required.

Examples include:

- content creation
- market research
- SEO optimisation
- website development
- competitor analysis
- product documentation
- PDMS support
- data analysis

The Planning Engine does not execute these skills.

It specifies when and why they should be invoked.

Output:

Skill invocation plan.

---

# Step 5 — Define Success Criteria

Every execution blueprint should include measurable success indicators.

Success criteria may include:

- business objectives achieved
- quality standards satisfied
- customer expectations met
- organisational standards followed
- measurable performance improvements

Success should be defined before execution begins.

Output:

Execution success criteria.

---

# Planning Quality

The Planning Engine should maximise:

- clarity
- feasibility
- efficiency
- organisational alignment
- adaptability
- execution readiness

A high-quality plan reduces uncertainty during execution.

---

# Success Criteria

A planning cycle is successful when:

- objectives are clearly defined
- strategy is appropriate
- execution steps are coherent
- required skills are identified
- success can be objectively evaluated
- execution can begin without significant ambiguity

---

# Relationship with Other Engines

The Planning Engine receives structured business judgement from the Business Judgement Engine.

Its output is an Execution Blueprint that guides the Execution Engine.

Planning does not perform execution.

Its responsibility is to ensure that execution begins with a coherent and validated plan.

---

# Philosophy

Scout does not plan to organise work.

Scout plans to improve execution.

The purpose of planning is to transform sound judgement into reliable business action, ensuring that every execution contributes to sustainable organisational capability rather than isolated task completion.
